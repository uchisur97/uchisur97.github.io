On Being Human

The Fire Within

Prologue.

28 July 2025✦4 min read

Kept only on this device.

Life is a system. A routine. A carefully constructed pattern that reflects the fire burning within me—an Aries to my core. That fire consumes me, but I never saw it as a flaw. Every goal I set, I crushed. Every obstacle, I bulldozed through. My days were a dance of precision and control, and for the longest time, that was enough.

I work in investment management—high stakes, endless calculations, invisible opponents. It’s not just a job; it’s a battlefield. My mornings begin in silence, alone at my desk, the only sound the click of keys as I sharpen my mind like a blade. Each spreadsheet, each presentation, feels like an extension of my will—calm, focused, but always brimming with potential energy, like the spark before the explosion.

Before the metro, I always stop at the café in my building. That ritual? Sacred. The hiss of steam, the clink of ceramic, the barista’s easy smile—it grounds me. “Another big day?” she teases. I nod. She knows me. My iced matcha slides across the counter, and for a second, there’s peace. This ritual isn’t about the caffeine; it’s about control. Predictability. Safety.

The metro ride is twelve minutes of rebellion. I don’t scroll—I observe. People. Movement. Patterns. In the quiet hum of the train, I disconnect from the digital world and reconnect with something real. Others are lost in screens. I look up. I see. It’s my way of resisting the pull of the world’s noise.

Amsterdam in the morning is raw and beautiful in equal parts. Beer-soaked streets, scattered trash, the clash of lives beginning and ending at once. But it’s mine. I walk those streets knowing I’m part of a city in motion, a silent soldier marching into another day.

The office is a contrast—clean lines, hushed voices, coffee-scented ambition. I smile at colleagues, engage in small talk that never touches the surface. I like the order here. The predictability. It’s all accounted for. Every folder, every meeting, every move. Mine.

The gym is where the fire lives. Therapy. Religion. Release. I suit up like a warrior—burgundy shorts, chalked hands, playlists full of fury. There’s a rhythm to the clang of barbells and the thud of dropped weights, but inside my headphones, it’s chaos. It matches the storm inside me.

Leg day is where I shine. The strongest. The most in control. But even here, I start to wonder. In the stillness between sets, in the moment the bar leaves the rack, a thought creeps in: What happens if this isn’t enough?

Fridays are for laundry. Folding shirts becomes folding thoughts. Sorting socks becomes sorting emotions. Every spin of the machine a quiet reset. Control.

Saturdays: gym and reading. Strength of body, strength of mind. The books I read plant questions I can’t always answer. Dreams bloom from their words—dreams that haunt my naps and whisper truths I’m not ready to face. What is my purpose? What am I chasing?

My routine isn’t just structure. It’s armor. A fortress against stagnation, against the spiral. When the productivity falters, the fear creeps in. Am I failing? Am I falling behind? The routine holds it all at bay.

I thought I built something impenetrable.

But cracks have formed.

Beneath the discipline, there’s a numbness I’ve long ignored. Not sadness. Not despair. Just a quiet emptiness that follows achievement. I don’t know when I stopped feeling the beauty in small things. It’s not that I’m unhappy—I’m just... incomplete. And I don’t know how long I’ve been this way.

I envy those who move slowly. Who drift through weekends and binge shows without guilt. People who scroll aimlessly or nap in the middle of the day. I watch them like you’d watch birds in flight—marveling at their freedom, knowing it’s not yours to claim.

I’m not built for breezes. I am fire. Always have been.

And love? Emotion? Those were never part of the plan. I studied them like artifacts behind glass. Never touched. Never let in. I built my life around isolation and called it discipline. I thought it made me strong. But the truth is, I wasn’t guarding myself. I was burying myself.

I became the villain in my own story.

Not by cruelty—but by absence. Absence of softness. Absence of stillness. Absence of me. The me who once craved more than numbers and control.

But I still had it. The illusion of control. And I held onto it like a sword. Because as long as the system worked, I could keep the darkness out.

Until him.

Miracle.

He didn’t crash into my world. He slipped into it—quietly, effortlessly. He didn’t try to match my fire. He simply held it, reflected it back, made me see it. See myself.

He didn’t challenge me. He stabilized me. Where I raged, he burned steady. And for the first time, I questioned the story I’d written about who I was.

He didn’t tame me.

He revealed how close I was to burning myself alive.

In him, I saw everything I had ignored. And in his presence, I felt something terrifying:

Hope.

But hope, like fire, can consume just as easily as it warms.

And when everything turned to ash, I had to ask:

What if fire could create instead of destroy?

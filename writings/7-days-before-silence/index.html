7 Days Before Silence

28 March 2026✦6 min read

Kept only on this device.

“The saddest thing about love is that not only that it cannot last forever, but that heartbreak is soon forgotten.”
~ William Faulkner

[somewhere in the past]

“So here I am—writing.

Not because I know what I feel, but because I don’t.

It’s strange, holding emotions that don’t have names. They move through me like shadows—pain, sadness, relief—layered over each other until I can’t tell where one ends and the other begins. I feel tired. Angry. Empty. And somehow, all of it at once.

I hate this feeling.
I hate not being in control.

There’s so much I still want to say to him. So many questions sitting in my throat, waiting. But I already know—if I push, he’ll pull away. So instead, I make a deal with myself.

One week.

One week to ask.
One week to be weak.
One week to love him without conditions.
And after that… I decide who I become.

Right now, I think I’ll become someone distant. Someone colder. And that thought scares me more than losing him.

Because I still believe—deep down—that we could have fixed it.

Valentine’s Day was supposed to be ours.

I had spent the entire week imagining it. Where we’d go. What he’d planned. Whether we’d eat somewhere nice or do something new together. I was busy—with school, with work—but in the background, there was always him.

While I was imagining us,
he was imagining how to leave.

That realization sits in my chest like something heavy and permanent.

I still wanted to see him.

I knew it was a bad idea.
I knew it would hurt.
I asked anyway.

We walked through Nieuwmarkt like nothing had changed.

He said he was hungry. I suggested food. We went to my favorite restaurant, and for a moment—it felt like a date. Like we had stepped backward in time.

Like we were still us.

And then we weren’t.

We left. Went our separate ways. Did our usual handshake. The one that always ended with a kiss.

This time, it didn’t.

And suddenly I couldn’t breathe.

I got into the metro, but the feeling followed me inside. So I got off early—Van der Made—and walked the rest of the way home.

I didn’t feel the cold.
Didn’t feel the rain.
Didn’t feel the pain in my heels.

Just… nothing.

I texted him that I was home, adding laughing emojis like I always do.

It’s funny—how you can be crying and still type “lol.”

At night, I called him again.

Out of habit. Out of hope. I don’t even know anymore.

I told him I wanted to sleep next to him. To end my day the way I always did. But even as I said it, I knew—

that version of us doesn’t exist anymore.

And every time I realize that, it feels like something inside me breaks all over again.

5 a.m.

Again.

I haven’t slept.

I lie there staring at the ceiling, trying to understand how someone can wake up one day and decide they don’t see a future with you anymore.

How do you look at someone with love in your eyes… and still be unhappy?

How do you hold that in for so long without trying to fix it?

That’s what makes me angry.

Of all the things that hurt, this is the worst—
the not knowing when it started ending.

He says he misses me.

But not enough.

It hurts him to see me in pain. That’s why he cried—not because he was losing me, but because he knew he was hurting me.

And somehow… that hurts more.

I start understanding things I wish I didn’t.

Maybe he didn’t fall out of love all at once. Maybe it happened slowly—while he was meeting new people, building a new version of his life.

And somewhere in that process… I stopped being someone he chose.

I became something he had.

Something expected.

Something… replaceable.

While I chose him every day,
he chose everything else.

There’s a moment where clarity hits, and I hate it.

Because I’m right.

And I hate being right about this.

Sunday is coming.

Decision day.

Do I keep him in my life as a friend—knowing I’ll always want more?
Or do I let him go completely—and lose him as a person too?

Both options hurt.

One just hurts longer.

9:08 a.m.

He will become a memory of what could have been.

A constant reminder of everything we didn’t get to do.

Holding hands.
Late-night talks.
Trips.
Cooking together.
Living together.

A whole future—gone.

And what replaces it?

Questions.

What went wrong?
Was I not enough?
Did he just give up?
Did I miss something?

I will always wonder.

And I hate that too.

Later, while cleaning my room, something shifts.

Not healing—just… a small, quiet realization.

Maybe I don’t have to decide everything right now.

Maybe I can just let it be.

Let time do what I can’t.

I make a reservation anyway.

Hotpot. 7:45.

If he says no, I’ll go alone.

Because I need an ending to this week. Something that feels like closure—even if it’s not.

I want to look at him one last time while I’m still in love.

He has a football match today.

I hope he wins.

And one day—when I read this again—

I hope I feel nothing at all.“

Four years later,

I read it again.

Not as her.

But as someone who once knew her.

The words are still heavy, still full of sharp edges and sleepless nights—but they don’t reach me anymore. They just… exist. Like a story I’ve read before. Like a character I understand deeply, but no longer recognize as myself.

I follow her thoughts carefully, almost gently. The way she spirals, the way she tries to make sense of something that refused to be understood. I see her clinging to answers that were never going to come, building meaning out of silence, turning absence into something personal.

And I don’t judge her.

I just don’t feel her.

My heart stays steady.

My breathing doesn’t change.

There are no tears waiting behind my eyes.

It’s quiet here.

⸻

I remember thinking that the pain would last forever.

That he would always be a “what if.”

That the questions would echo.

That I would never stop wondering.

But I did.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. No big moment, no final goodbye that fixed everything. It just… faded. Piece by piece, day by day, until one day there was nothing left to carry.

And I didn’t even notice when it happened.

⸻

What surprises me now isn’t that it ended.

It’s how much of myself I had placed inside of him.

How I believed that losing him meant losing something essential. As if he held a version of me I couldn’t access on my own. As if love, once given, couldn’t be taken back and reshaped.

But it can.

I took it back.

I rebuilt myself in ways she couldn’t imagine yet. In ways she didn’t even know she needed.

⸻

She thought she needed answers.

She didn’t.

Distance. Time.

Space to become someone who no longer asked those questions.

⸻

Now, reading this.

I feel… recognition.

Like passing by an old house you once lived in. You remember where everything used to be—the couch, the window, the place where you cried—but there’s no need to walk back inside.

I’m in a new home now.

⸻

Which is strange.

He who was once everything.

Became just a chapter.

And I—

I am the author who kept writing.

On Being Human·Poem

Between Fear and Freedom

15 September 2025✦3 min read

Kept only on this device.

“I learned that courage was not the absence of fear, but the triumph over it. The brave man is not he who does not feel afraid, but he who conquers that fear.”
~ Nelson Mandela

I’m in bed.
Staring at the ceiling that feels like it’s sinking lower.
The smell of a candle just blown out still hangs in the air,
a faint trace of smoke,
sharp and sweet.
My blanket is warm,
too warm,
like it wants to trap me instead of comfort me.
The TV flickers across the room,
a Korean drama spilling voices and music
I can’t really follow.
The sound is company.
The light is distraction.

I’ve been scared.
Terrified.
The past hours.
The past days.
The past weeks.
My head screaming.
Everything a blur of chaos.

I’ve slept too much.
Eaten too much.
Trained too little.
A complete mess.

And in that mess,
I’ve been brutal with myself.
Picking apart every flaw.
Every failure.
But doing nothing to change it.

The pattern is familiar.
The same feelings.
The same smells in the air.
The same cycle, repeating.

I know how to change it.
I’ve always known.
But knowing is never the problem.
Doing is.

Courage doesn’t live in the mind.
It lives in action.
And action is messy.
Uncontrolled.
Terrifying.

Without a plan, without money,
without the safety of being at my peak,
failure feels unbearable.
Poisonous.

I came across a proverb —
or maybe I misremember it.
It said something like:
We don’t avoid filth because it’s dirty —
because dirt can be cleaned.
We avoid it because we fear the poison within.

I took it to mean:
if something is scary and you know it,
it’ll be okay.

Maybe fear isn’t the enemy.
Maybe it’s the signpost.
The signal that I’m standing
at the threshold of growth.

And I want to talk about it.
To let the words out.
To let someone in.
But the thought of people worrying about me—
it makes my skin crawl.

I’m supposed to be the strong one.
The anchor.
The hero.
How could I falter
when so many things demand my strength?

And yet here I am.
Again.
Same pattern.
Same weight.

The blanket presses heavier on my chest.
The TV’s voices fade into background static.
The air feels thick,
like it’s listening.

I have the discipline.
I know what to do.
I’ve proven it before.
What I lack
is consistency.

So I ask myself:
what does it take this time?
What will it take?
How many cycles until I break through
instead of breaking down?

What makes a person free?

Not in the grand, political sense.
Not in the abstract way philosophers debate.
But in the quiet way
that lives between my ribs.

I don’t want freedom from responsibility.
Or from effort.
Or from struggle.

I want freedom from fear’s weight on my chest.
From the whispers that say I’m not enough.
From the thoughts that run me in circles.
From the voices that don’t belong to me,
but echo inside me anyway.

I want freedom from perfectionism.
From seeing failure as final.
From believing one stumble erases me.

I want freedom.
To try.
To stumble.
To rise again.

To live without fear as a cage—
but as a signal.
A reminder that I am alive.
That I am daring.
That I am moving.

Maybe freedom isn’t the absence of fear.
Maybe freedom is the courage
to carry it
without letting it chain me.

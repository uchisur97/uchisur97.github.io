Fragments of Thought

The Garden We Share

She grew where the weather softened. I know because I remember when it didn’t

26 September 2026✦4 min read

Kept only on this device.

“Do the best you can until you know better. Then when you know better, do better.”
~ Maya Angelou

There were years when the sky hung low over our house, when every room had its own climate and I learned to read pressure before I learned to call it fear. I became good at standing beneath things, at mistaking endurance for strength, at believing that because I was being struck by the weather, I could not also become part of it.

I know better now.

For a long time, I told myself I had stood between her and the storm. There was truth in that. Sometimes I was the tree that broke the wind before it reached her. Sometimes I was the branch that broke beneath it and fell in her direction.

I carried so much thunder inside me that I stopped noticing when others began to flinch at the sound. I had learned that pain arrived without permission, and without meaning to, I sometimes taught her the same lesson.

It was survival.

Survival is an explanation, not an eraser.

Some ashes settle where they were never meant to fall. Some were mine. I know some settled on her. The fire burning through me did not make them lighter.

Memory is generous to the wounded. For years, I remembered what I endured so vividly that I nearly mistook my suffering for innocence.

Pain does not make the hands harmless.

Love does not either.

I loved her then.

I hurt her then.

Both are true.

And still, I see her now: at the table, in the light, laughing as though the walls have finally forgotten how to close around us. Something ancient inside me goes quiet.

I used to think her softness would make me bitter. Sometimes I still mourn the ease I was never given. There are gardens you can love and still grieve for never having been allowed to bloom in.

I once looked at her blooming and saw proof of my protection. I see it differently now. Some things grow because we tend them. Some grow despite us. Some learn to turn toward the sun after years of bending around our shadows.

I do not know which parts of her belong to which truth.

That knowing is hers.

Her life belongs to her. Her grace is her own. Her softness is her own. I do not get to gather the beautiful things she became and arrange them into evidence of my goodness.

She is simply herself.

And I am glad.

Her laughter fills rooms that once felt heavy. I hear it and feel something loosen inside me. I have learned to let that sound belong to her without turning it into proof that I saved something.

Perhaps that is another kind of love: to stop making yourself the author of someone else’s survival.

I know better than to call myself her shelter. Shelters do not shake. Shelters do not sometimes become weather themselves.

There are marks in our shared earth that belong to my hands. Flowers have grown around them. The marks remain.

I cannot return to those seasons with gentler hands.

The garden remembers.

Maybe she does too.

There may be wounds I have made smaller in memory because I was never the one who had to carry them. If she names them, I want to hear them at their true size. I want to resist reaching for the story of what was done to me when she is speaking about what I did to her.

Remorse and repair are different things.

Regret happens inside me.

Repair must make room for her.

I cannot change by asking the past to recognize who I am now. I cannot make forgiveness the measure of whether I have become better.

I simply become different.

Again and again.

I tend to what is still alive. I pull what I can from my own roots before it reaches across the soil. I learn the shape of my storms early enough to close the windows myself.

Love is sometimes admitting that someone once needed shelter from you.

Sometimes it is becoming someone they no longer need shelter from.

And she—

she keeps growing.

Beside me.

We came from the same soil. We learned different ways of reaching through it.

She reached for the sun.

I learned first how to survive the rain.

Eventually, I learned to recognize the rain in myself.

There is a garden between us now.

I do not know what lies beneath all of it. Some roots have tangled so deeply I cannot tell where one begins and another ends. There are places I am afraid to disturb. There are things that may surface years from now, carried upward by a season neither of us can see coming.

I cannot know what she will remember.

I cannot know what she will one day ask of me.

So I keep my hands open.

Some mornings, there are new leaves.

Some mornings, there is only earth.

I am learning not to call either one forgiveness.

For now, she is beside me.

For now, there is still something here to tend.

The weather changes.

I watch what it does.
